# Attendance Policy

## Minimum Attendance Requirement

All students enrolled in undergraduate and postgraduate programs must maintain a minimum attendance of 75% in each subject throughout the semester. Attendance is calculated separately for theory classes, practical sessions, and tutorials.

## Attendance Tracking

Attendance is recorded through biometric systems installed in classrooms and laboratories. Students are required to mark their attendance at the beginning of each session. If the biometric system is not functioning, the faculty member will take manual attendance.

## Consequences of Low Attendance

Students whose attendance falls below 75% in any subject will be issued a warning letter after the mid-semester review. If attendance remains below 75% at the end of the semester, the student may be barred from appearing in the end-semester examination for that subject.

Students barred from examinations due to attendance shortage will be marked as "Detained" in the subject and must re-register for it in the following semester.

## Attendance Relaxation

Students who miss classes due to medical emergencies, participation in college-approved events, or sports activities at the university or national level may apply for attendance relaxation. The application must be submitted to the Head of Department within 7 days of returning to class, along with supporting documents such as a medical certificate or event participation letter.

A maximum relaxation of 10% may be granted, meaning the effective minimum attendance after relaxation is 65%.

## Medical Leave

Students who are absent due to illness for 3 or more consecutive days must submit a medical certificate from a registered medical practitioner within 5 working days of resuming classes. The certificate must include the diagnosis, the period of rest, and the doctor's registration number.

## Communication to Parents

If a student's attendance falls below 60% in any subject, the college will send a formal letter to the parent or guardian informing them of the shortage.
